Strange Request

Productivity for procrastinators (experimental)

Anonymous tip. A stranger rewords every note.

What this is

A to-do list that assumes you can't fully trust a plan your earlier self made.

Built on John Perry's structured procrastination: keep one important task at the top and get everything else done as a way of avoiding it. Each note is treated as a message from a stranger, your past self, who wrote it in some mood with a guess about today. Review it before acting on it.

How it works

Feeling and urgency
Record the mood a note was written in, so today's you can discount it. Plans are made calm and carried out tired (Loewenstein's hot-cold empathy gap).
Deadlines and the score line
We underestimate how long things take (the planning fallacy). Finished notes compare deadline with actual day, so you see how reliable your deadlines are.
Open and Decoy
Your top note stays Open, dimmed. The next is the Decoy, promoted as the thing to do first. People drift to what is nearer and easier (Steel), so the desk uses the drift.
Push down and Amend
Push down admits a note isn't happening today. Amend rewrites it with the old wording struck through. Our preferences shift as action nears (Ainslie), so you renegotiate with your earlier self.
Case notes
Finishing anything but the top note is logged as work done instead. Check whether the avoidance pays off, and whether pushed-down notes ever mattered.
Anonymous tip
Optional. Claude rewords every note as if someone else wrote it. A hunch, not a finding: unfamiliar words may loosen your attachment to the first version. Only appears where the page can reach Claude.

What it does not claim

Nobody has tested whether this reduces procrastination. The links to the research are design reasoning, and the research describes general tendencies, not what will work for you. It's a toy and an experiment, not therapy or a clinical tool. It makes no claims about trauma or clinical conditions.

Your data

Everything is saved in this browser only. Clearing browser data erases it. If you switch on Anonymous tip, your note text is sent to Claude to be reworded, using your own Claude usage, after the page asks permission. Clear examples in the menu removes the sample notes.
